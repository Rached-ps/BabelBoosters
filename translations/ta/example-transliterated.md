# Tamil — written in Latin letters

> **This is an example file, not a real person's contribution.** It is here to
> show what a finished submission looks like when the language is written in
> Latin letters. Please do not edit it — make your own file at
> `translations/ta/<your-github-username>.md`.

**This file is a complete contribution.** It is written in Tamil, using Latin
letters instead of the Tamil script, and it counts exactly the same as one
written in தமிழ். Millions of people write Tamil this way every day, in
messages and searches. If that is how you write your language, write it that
way here.

- **Language code:** `ta`
- **Script:** Latin letters (transliterated Tamil)
- **GitHub username:** *(example file — your file goes under your own username)*
- **Anything you want to say about your language or your choices:**
  I have written the Tamil word in Latin letters first, then given the Tamil
  script in brackets for anyone who can read it. You do not have to do the
  second part — I have only added it so the two example files can be compared
  side by side. There is no single correct way to spell Tamil in Latin letters:
  *kilai*, *kiḷai* and *kilay* are all the same word. Spell it the way you would
  type it to a friend.

## The twelve words

### 1. open source

*Software whose code anyone can read, use, change and share.*

- **Your language:** thirandha moolam (திறந்த மூலம்)
- **Note (optional):** *thirandha* means open, *moolam* means source or origin.
  It is a real translation and it is understood, but in practice most people
  just say "open source" in the middle of a Tamil sentence.

### 2. repository

*The folder that holds a project's files, and the history of every change made
to them.*

- **Your language:** kalanjiyam (களஞ்சியம்)
- **Note (optional):** *kalanjiyam* means a granary or storehouse — the place
  where the harvest is kept. It is the established Tamil word for a repository
  and the image works nicely.

### 3. issue

*A written note describing a bug, a question, or an idea for a project.*

- **Your language:** sikkal (சிக்கல்)
- **Note (optional):** *sikkal* means a knot or a tangle, so it fits a problem
  well. In speech people usually say *pirachanai* (பிரச்சனை), which means
  problem. Both lean towards "something is wrong", so neither quite covers an
  issue that is just an idea.

### 4. fork

*Your own copy of someone else's repository, kept under your account, which you
can change freely.*

- **Your language:** fork
- **Note (optional):** There is no settled Tamil word for this. Tamil has
  *kavaru* for a forked branch, but nobody uses it in this sense and it would
  not be understood. Everyone says "fork". Leaving it as it is, rather than
  inventing something, is the honest answer.

### 5. clone

*A copy of a repository downloaded onto your own computer.*

- **Your language:** nagal (நகல்)
- **Note (optional):** *nagal* means a copy or duplicate — the word used for a
  photocopy of a document. In conversation people say "clone pannu".

### 6. branch

*A separate line of work, so you can change things without affecting the main
version.*

- **Your language:** kilai (கிளை)
- **Note (optional):** A branch of a tree, and also a branch of a bank. Same
  picture as in English, so it needs no explaining at all.

### 7. commit

*One saved change, with a short message explaining what you did.*

- **Your language:** commit
- **Note (optional):** No settled Tamil word. *sethippu* or *panivu* have been
  suggested but are not in use anywhere. People say "commit pannu" — the English
  noun with a Tamil verb attached, which is how a lot of technical Tamil works.

### 8. pull request

*A request asking a project to take your changes into its main version.*

- **Your language:** izhukkum korikkai (இழுக்கும் கோரிக்கை)
- **Note (optional):** *izhu* is to pull, *korikkai* is a request, so this is a
  faithful translation and useful when explaining what a PR is. But nobody says
  it out loud — in speech it is "pull request", or "PR".

### 9. review

*When someone reads your proposed changes and gives feedback before they are
accepted.*

- **Your language:** marupaarvai (மறுபார்வை)
- **Note (optional):** Literally "looking again" — *maru* is again, *paarvai*
  is a look. Well established and immediately clear.

### 10. merge

*Combining accepted changes into the project's main version.*

- **Your language:** inaippu (இணைப்பு)
- **Note (optional):** *inai* is to join, so *inaippu* is a joining or union.
  The verb form is *inaikka*. Spoken, "merge pannu".

### 11. release

*A version of the project packaged up and published for people to use.*

- **Your language:** veliyeedu (வெளியீடு)
- **Note (optional):** *veli* means outside, so *veliyeedu* is a bringing-out —
  the same word used for publishing a book or releasing a film. It transfers to
  software very naturally.

### 12. maintainer

*A person who looks after a project — reviewing, merging and guiding it.*

- **Your language:** paramarippaalar (பராமரிப்பாளர்)
- **Note (optional):** *paramari* is to look after or care for, and the *-aalar*
  ending makes it the person who does it. It is a long word but a warm one — it
  is the word you would use for someone who looks after a garden, or a person.

## The two sentences

### Sentence 1

> Accelerate is a student club at Manipal University Jaipur that helps people
> make their first contribution to open source.

- **Your language:**
  Accelerate enbadhu Manipal University Jaipur-in oru maanavar kazhagam. Idhu,
  makkal thangaludaiya mudhal pangalippai open source-il seivadharku
  udhavugiradhu.
- **Note (optional):** Tamil does not join this as comfortably as English does,
  so I have split it into two sentences. *enbadhu* marks a name being
  introduced, roughly "the thing called". I kept "open source" in English here
  because that is what a student would actually hear said.

### Sentence 2

> You don't need to be an expert to contribute. Everyone starts with one small
> change.

- **Your language:**
  Pangalippu seiya neengal nipunaraga irukka vendiyadhillai. Ovvoruvarum oru
  siriya maatrathil irundhe thodangugiraargal.
- **Note (optional):** *neengal* is the polite "you", which is right for
  speaking to someone you have just met. The *-e* on *irundhe* adds emphasis —
  "everyone starts from just one small change" — which carries the reassurance
  of the English sentence.
