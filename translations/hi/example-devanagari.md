- **Anything you want to say about your language or your choices:**
  Hindi has two registers running side by side here. There is a formal,
  Sanskrit-derived vocabulary used in official documents and in software
  translated into Hindi, and there is the way developers actually talk, which
  borrows heavily from English. I have given the formal word first where one
  genuinely exists, and said in the note what people say out loud. Neither one
  is the "real" Hindi — a beginner will meet both.

## The twelve words

### 1. open source

*Software whose code anyone can read, use, change and share.*

- **Your language:** मुक्त स्रोत (mukt srot) / ओपन सोर्स (open source)
- **Note (optional):** मुक्त स्रोत is the literal translation — मुक्त means
  free, स्रोत means source — and it is used in writing. In conversation almost
  everybody says ओपन सोर्स.

### 2. repository

*The folder that holds a project's files, and the history of every change made
to them.*

- **Your language:** भंडार (bhandar) / रिपॉज़िटरी (repository)
- **Note (optional):** भंडार means "store" or "storehouse" and is the word used
  in Hindi software translations. Spoken, people say रिपॉज़िटरी, or just रेपो.

### 3. issue

*A written note describing a bug, a question, or an idea for a project.*

- **Your language:** मुद्दा (mudda)
- **Note (optional):** समस्या (problem) is the more obvious choice but it is too
  narrow — a GitHub issue can be an idea or a question, not only something
  wrong. मुद्दा means "matter" or "point raised", which covers all three.

### 4. fork

*Your own copy of someone else's repository, kept under your account, which you
can change freely.*

- **Your language:** फ़ोर्क (fork)
- **Note (optional):** There is no settled Hindi word for this. The literal
  translation of fork is काँटा, which is the thing you eat with — it carries
  none of the meaning here and would confuse a reader. Everybody says फ़ोर्क.

### 5. clone

*A copy of a repository downloaded onto your own computer.*

- **Your language:** प्रतिलिपि (pratilipi) / क्लोन (clone)
- **Note (optional):** प्रतिलिपि means "copy" and is well understood, though it
  misses the sense of a copy that stays connected to the original. क्लोन is what
  people say.

### 6. branch

*A separate line of work, so you can change things without affecting the main
version.*

- **Your language:** शाखा (shakha)
- **Note (optional):** This one is easy. शाखा is already used for a branch of a
  tree and a branch of a bank, so the image carries over exactly. Everyone
  understands it straight away.

### 7. commit

*One saved change, with a short message explaining what you did.*

- **Your language:** कमिट (commit)
- **Note (optional):** No settled Hindi word. सहेजना means "to save", which is
  close, but it loses the important part — that a commit is recorded
  permanently, with a message, as one step in a history. I have kept कमिट
  rather than invent something.

### 8. pull request

*A request asking a project to take your changes into its main version.*

- **Your language:** पुल रिक्वेस्ट (pull request)
- **Note (optional):** When explaining it to someone, people often say
  "बदलाव का प्रस्ताव" — a proposal for a change — and that is a good
  explanation. But it is not what anyone calls it. In speech it is पुल रिक्वेस्ट,
  or just पीआर.

### 9. review

*When someone reads your proposed changes and gives feedback before they are
accepted.*

- **Your language:** समीक्षा (sameeksha)
- **Note (optional):** Long established — used for book and film reviews too.
  Reads naturally and needs no explanation.

### 10. merge

*Combining accepted changes into the project's main version.*

- **Your language:** विलय (vilay) / मर्ज करना (merge karna)
- **Note (optional):** विलय is the standard word, used for company mergers and
  for rivers meeting. In conversation people say मर्ज करना.

### 11. release

*A version of the project packaged up and published for people to use.*

- **Your language:** रिलीज़ (release) / संस्करण जारी करना (sanskaran jaari karna)
- **Note (optional):** संस्करण जारी करना means "to issue a version" and is the
  accurate description, but it is a phrase rather than a name for the thing.
  As a noun, people say रिलीज़.

### 12. maintainer

*A person who looks after a project — reviewing, merging and guiding it.*

- **Your language:** अनुरक्षक (anurakshak) / मेंटेनर (maintainer)
- **Note (optional):** अनुरक्षक is the formal word and appears in Hindi software
  translations; अनुरक्षण means upkeep or maintenance. It is a real word but many
  people meet it for the first time here. Spoken, it is मेंटेनर.

## The two sentences

### Sentence 1

> Accelerate is a student club at Manipal University Jaipur that helps people
> make their first contribution to open source.

- **Your language:**
  Accelerate मणिपाल यूनिवर्सिटी जयपुर का एक छात्र क्लब है, जो लोगों को ओपन सोर्स
  में उनका पहला योगदान देने में मदद करता है।
- **Note (optional):** I have left "Accelerate" in Latin letters because it is a
  name. Hindi says योगदान देना (to give a contribution), not योगदान करना, which
  is a common slip.

### Sentence 2

> You don't need to be an expert to contribute. Everyone starts with one small
> change.

- **Your language:**
  योगदान देने के लिए विशेषज्ञ होना ज़रूरी नहीं है। हर कोई एक छोटे बदलाव से ही
  शुरुआत करता है।
- **Note (optional):** The ही in the second sentence has no direct English
  equivalent — it adds a gentle "only", so it reads as "everyone starts with
  just one small change". It makes the sentence warmer, which felt right here.
